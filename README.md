# How-does-U.S.-driving-activity-respond-to-changes-in-gasoline-prices-
This project estimates the relationship between U.S. vehicle miles traveled and gasoline prices. Understanding how drivers shift their behavior in response to changes in gasoline prices is crucial for evaluating the effectiveness of carbon taxes. If gasoline demand is inelastic, price-based climate policies may not reduce gas emissions. A log-log model was chosen to facilitate elasticity calculations, and real GDP was included to control for macroeconomic conditions. The results suggest that small price changes have little effect on vehicle miles traveled, while higher gasoline prices lead to a significant reduction in driving.
