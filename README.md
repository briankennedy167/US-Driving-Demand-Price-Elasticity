# Estimating the Price Elasticity of U.S. Driving Demand

## Project Overview

This project examines the relationship between U.S. vehicle miles
traveled (VMT) and gasoline prices using econometric analysis in Python.

The research investigates how driving behavior responds to changes
in fuel prices and evaluates the implications for energy demand,
consumer behavior, and environmental policy.

## Research Objectives

- Estimate the price elasticity of U.S. driving demand.
- Examine nonlinear relationships between gasoline prices and driving activity.
- Control for macroeconomic conditions using real GDP.
- Evaluate the implications for price-based environmental policies.

## Econometric Methodology

- Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)
- Log-Log Regression
- Nonlinear Regression (Quadratic Specification)
- Macroeconomic Control Variables

## Data

The analysis incorporates three economic variables:

- U.S. Vehicle Miles Traveled (VMT)
- U.S. Regular Conventional Gasoline Prices
- Real Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

## Key Findings

- The relationship between gasoline prices and driving activity is nonlinear.
- Estimated price elasticity becomes more negative as gasoline prices rise.
- Real GDP is positively associated with driving activity.
- The final model explains approximately 91.7% of the variation
  in monthly vehicle miles traveled.
- All reported slope coefficients are statistically significant
  at the 1% level.
